Discussion The most important finding is that knowledge base quality outweighs model quality at this scale. The v2 chunking improvement (+0.292 keyword coverage) is nearly four times larger than the QLoRA fine-tuning gain (+0.079). Getting the data processing right mattered more than model selection. The second finding is that the XAI confidence signals trade coverage for honesty. Removing source agreement raises raw keyword coverage by 0.061 points, which looks like an improvement on paper. In practice it means the system stops flagging responses where retrieved sources conflict, and those conflicts are exactly the cases where a medical qa system should communicate uncertainty rather than silently pass through an answer. BioMistral generates semantically better answers by ROUGE-L and BERTScore, but is 35 % slower than TinyLlama on CPU (143 s vs. 106 s mean). For a deployment prioritising answer quality over latency, BioMistral is the better choice. For a CPU-constrained environment, TinyLlama is more practical. The architecture was designed so that swapping backends requires changing a single configuration parameter. The retrieval hit proxy of 97.2 % on 36 end-to-end questions is encouraging, though the test set is small. The one miss was a rare surgical technique question, pointing to a gap in the knowledge base: the corpus indexes clinical conversation and research abstracts well, but narrow surgical procedure literature is underrepresented.
